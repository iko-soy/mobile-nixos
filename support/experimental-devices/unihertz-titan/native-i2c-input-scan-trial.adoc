= Touch and keyboard bus scan trial

Status on 2026-10-17: *proposed; not run.* Running it needs the owner's
explicit approval of this exact procedure, and that approval covers one run.

The link:native-i2c-scan-trial.adoc[I2C bus scan trial] and the
identification trials that followed enabled the sensors' I2C controller at
`0x11011000` through a kexec'd tree and scanned it. This trial does the same
for two other controllers at once: `0x11007000`, the vendor's bus 0, where the
overlay places the touch panel, and `0x11008000`, the vendor's bus 4, where it
places the keyboard's controller and the cameras. Each bus is scanned once with
reads only. No device node is added, so no device driver binds and nothing is
written to any device. The image is the one of the
link:native-i2c-id-repeat-trial.adoc[I2C identification repeat trial],
unchanged.

== Questions

. Does the kexec with both controllers enabled pass its checks and boot with
  all eight CPUs and the same reserved memory, and do exactly two adapters
  appear, `i2c-0` for the controller at `11007000` and `i2c-4` for the one at
  `11008000`?
. Are each bus's pins in function 1 with both lines high: GPIO82 and GPIO83
  (`SDA0`, `SCL0`) for bus 0, GPIO105 and GPIO106 (`SCL4`, `SDA4`) for bus 4?
. Which addresses answer one read-only scan of each bus, compared with the
  overlay's `0x5d` on bus 0 and `0x58`, `0x5a`, `0x10`, `0x35`, `0x36`,
  `0x72`, `0x51` and `0x54` on bus 4, and is each a real result: finished in
  under two seconds, with the controller's interrupt count risen by exactly
  224 less the number of answers, two for each missing answer and one for
  each answer?
. Are both lines of each bus released after its scan?
. Does `titan-session end-in 1` restart the phone into Ubuntu Touch, and does
  the restore work as before?

== Basis

* *Controllers.* The native tree's nodes match the vendor's for both: the
  same register block, the same DMA channel (`0x11000080` for bus 0,
  `0x11000100` for bus 4) and the same interrupt (81 and 82). The native
  clocks are those of mainline's `mt8183.dtsi`: `CLK_INFRA_I2C0` and
  `CLK_INFRA_AP_DMA` for bus 0, and `CLK_INFRA_I2C1`, `CLK_INFRA_AP_DMA` and
  `CLK_INFRA_I2C1_ARBITER` for bus 4; the vendor's clock numbers are one
  higher throughout, as for bus 1. The aliases name them `i2c0` and `i2c4`.
  Neither node sets `clock-frequency`, so the driver runs each bus at
  100 kHz; the overlay asks for 400 kHz.
* *Bus 4's channels.* The vendor's driver reaches bus 4's registers at an
  offset of `0x100` and gives the camera control unit `0x200`, behind an
  arbiter whose clock the native node enables; why it avoids offset 0 is not
  documented. Mainline has no channel offsets and uses offset 0, as
  mainline's MT8183 boards do with this controller: Jacuzzi's display bridge
  at `0x58` and the EEPROMs at `0x50` of Kakadu, Krane and Kodama sit on it.
  That channel 0's interrupt and DMA handshake reach the main CPU is shown on
  MT8183, not on MT6771; if they do not, each transfer stalls for two
  seconds, which the scan check catches. The driver writes only its own
  channel's registers and its own DMA channel, at probe and after each
  missing answer, and the camera control unit does not run here.
* *Pins.* Mainline's MT8183 pin table gives `SDA0` and `SCL0` to GPIO82 and
  GPIO83 and `SCL4` and `SDA4` to GPIO105 and GPIO106, all in function 1, and
  no other pins for these buses. Ubuntu Touch's GPIO table, read on
  2026-10-16, shows all four in mode 1, as inputs reading high.
* *Devices in the vendor overlay.*
** Bus 0: the touch panel at `0x5d` (`mediatek,cap_touch`), with its
   interrupt on GPIO1 and its reset on GPIO18; Ubuntu Touch drives GPIO18
   high, out of reset.
** Bus 4: the keyboard's controller, an Awinic AW9523 at `0x58`, with its
   reset on GPIO169 and its interrupt on GPIO2; a second AW9523 at `0x5a`,
   for LEDs; the cameras at `0x10` and `0x35`, the first camera's focus motor
   at `0x72`, the cameras' EEPROMs at `0x51` and `0x54`, and a node of the
   camera control unit at `0x36`. Ubuntu Touch drives GPIO169 high, out of
   reset.
+
Whether the touch panel and the AW9523s answer depends on their supplies and
reset lines in the kexec'd kernel, which no driver sets; the session reads
GPIO1, GPIO2, GPIO18 and GPIO169 to tell. The cameras are powered only while
in use and are not expected to answer.
* *What a scan does.* As in the scan trial, `i2cdetect -y -r` reads one byte
  from each address from `0x08` to `0x77` and writes nothing; this controller
  cannot send a write without data. A device that answers returns a byte from
  its current register, usually the first one after reset. On the AW9523
  that is input port 0, which mainline's `pinctrl-aw9523` driver reads in its
  interrupt handler to clear the chip's interrupt; the scan's read may do
  the same. Its chip ID, `0x23` at register `0x10`, is for a later trial.
* *Image.* The repeat trial's, unchanged: flash profile `ram-nixos-10`, image
  `f37e2137...`, padded `f79e3eb4...`, with the same kernel, stage 0, store
  and kexec payload. It has the static `i2cdetect`; `titan-i2c-id` refuses
  without the controller at `11011000` and is not run.
* *Checks.* The session's tree check now requires the modified tree to
  differ from the running one in exactly the two nodes' `status`, from
  `disabled` to `okay`, both in the `dtc` sources and property by property,
  with the same reserve map. It passes a tree changed that way with
  `fdtput` and refuses one with a third change. The scan check accepts the
  recorded scans of bus 1 and refuses a constructed scan with one stall, or
  with an interrupt miscounted; the scan step refuses to run before the
  adapter check, twice on a bus, or on bus 4 before bus 0 is settled. An
  independent review of this procedure and its scripts found no blocker;
  its findings are fixed.

== Preconditions

As in the identification repeat trial, with the latest control-region copy now
the one from its Part 4.

== Procedure

As in the link:native-i2c-scan-trial.adoc[I2C bus scan trial], with flash
profile `ram-nixos-10` and the image
`native-ram-image-v13/UNTESTED-UNSIGNED-native-ram.img`, whose step 7
read-back must show `f79e3eb4...`, and with these changes:

* *Step 11:* set `status` to `okay` on `/soc/i2c@11007000` and
  `/soc/i2c@11008000`, each checked with `fdtget` before and after, and pass
  the tree check above; the marker is `titan_kexec=i2c04`. The records must
  show both nodes reading `okay` and a tree that differs from the one handed
  over only in `/chosen`.
* *Step 12, adapters:* `ls /sys/bus/i2c/devices` must show exactly `i2c-0`
  and `i2c-4`, and `readlink -f` must end in `/11007000.i2c/i2c-0` and
  `/11008000.i2c/i2c-4`.
* *Step 12, pins:* save the lines for GPIO1, GPIO2, GPIO18, GPIO82, GPIO83,
  GPIO105, GPIO106 and GPIO169 from the pinctrl debugfs file. A bus may be
  scanned only if both its pins are in function 1, by MediaTek's rule, and
  read input level 1.
* *Step 12, scans:* bus 0 first, then bus 4. Just before each scan its
  bus's pins are read again and must pass; bus 4 is scanned only if bus 0
  was refused by its gate or gave a real result with all four lines high
  afterwards. Save the controller's line of `/proc/interrupts`, run `timeout
  300 i2cdetect -y -r 0` or `-r 4` once, then save the interrupt line, the
  eight pin lines and the kernel log. A real result finishes with status 0
  in under two seconds, with the interrupt count risen by exactly 224 less
  the number of answers, at most eight answers and no I2C error logged, and
  the bus's lines must read 1 afterwards. No other I2C command.
* *Part 4:* after the control-region copy, the owner checks in Ubuntu Touch
  that the touch screen, the keyboard and its backlight work. If one does
  not, the phone is powered fully off once, which resets the devices on
  these buses, and checked again; either way it is recorded.

== Stop conditions

The scan trial's table applies, with these rows replacing its adapter, pin and
scan rows:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Observation |Action
|The adapters are not exactly `i2c-0` and `i2c-4` for these controllers
|Record the kernel log and go to the end step without a scan.
|The tree differs by more than the two status lines, `titan-kexec` refuses
or the load fails, or a segment fails the placement check
|Record the output and go to the end step. Never run `kexec` by hand.
|The kexec'd kernel's tree does not read `okay` for both nodes, or differs
from the tree handed over outside `/chosen`
|Record it and go to the end step without a scan.
|One bus's pins are not in function 1, or one of its lines reads 0
|Record the pin lines and skip that bus's scan; the other bus may be scanned.
|After a scan, a line of either bus reads 0
|A device holds the bus. Record the lines, run no further I2C command, go to
the end step, and after Part 3 power the phone fully off once.
|A scan is not a real result: it failed, took two seconds or more, counted
other than 224 less the number of answers, showed more than eight addresses
or logged an I2C error
|Record it, run no further I2C command and go to the end step.
|A scan has not finished after five minutes
|End with `titan-session end-in 1` if SSH answers; otherwise the session
deadlines restart the phone. Then Part 3.
|===

An empty table from bus 4 while GPIO169 reads high would point at the
controller's channel 0 rather than a missing keyboard controller. On bus 4,
more than eight answers with both lines high could also be powered camera
parts; the table is recorded as it stands, and the trial still stops there.

== Residual risks

As in the scan trial, now on two buses. The keyboard controller's pending
input interrupt may be cleared by the scan's read. A device without power can
load a bus's lines; the pin check before each scan catches lines held low. A
read that times out ends without a stop condition and the driver has no bus
recovery; the line check after each scan shows whether that left a device
holding the bus, and stops further scans.

== What this can and cannot show

Success shows which devices answer on the touch panel's and the keyboard's
buses in the native kernel, the start for their drivers. It does not show a
driver, the touch panel or the keyboard working, or the display.
