= Keyboard bus channel trial

Status on 2026-10-17: *proposed; not run.* Running it needs the owner's
explicit approval of this exact procedure, and that approval covers one run.

In the link:native-i2c-input-scan-trial.adoc[touch and keyboard bus scan
trial] the I2C controller at `0x11008000`, the vendor's bus 4, completed no
transfer: every address stalled for its two-second timeout without an
interrupt. That controller is shared with the camera control unit, and
MediaTek's kernel drives it differently from mainline: it resets it through
channel 0, then drives it through the channel at `0x100`, whose own copy of
the registers below `0x100` it programs before each transfer, and on that
channel it clears the multi-channel FIFO address, takes a missing
acknowledgement from the status when the transfer completes, and routes the
channel's interrupt to the CPU. Mainline drove channel 0 as a whole
controller and did none of this. Kernel patch 0006 adds MediaTek's handling
to mainline's driver for a node with the property `mediatek,channel-offset`.
This trial enables bus 4 alone with that property set to `0x100` and scans
it once with reads only.

== Questions

. Does the kexec with bus 4 enabled through its channel pass its checks and
  boot with all eight CPUs and the same reserved memory, and does exactly
  one adapter appear, `i2c-4` for the controller at `11008000`?
. Are GPIO105 and GPIO106 in function 1 with both lines high?
. Does the scan now give a real result: finished in under two seconds, with
  the controller's interrupt count risen by exactly 112, one per address?
  Which addresses answer?
. Are both lines released after the scan?
. Does `titan-session end-in 1` restart the phone into Ubuntu Touch, and does
  the restore work as before?

== Basis

* *MediaTek's driver.* MediaTek's MT6771 kernel, as published in vendor
  trees for other MT6771 phones (`drivers/i2c/busses/i2c-mtk.c`; a revision
  that differs from the Titan's in details, such as the name of its camera
  channel property), takes `ch_offset_default = <0x100>` for bus 4 from the
  vendor's base tree and adds it to every controller register it touches
  while transferring; its reset at probe and after a timeout goes to
  channel 0, with that channel's interrupts masked. Before each transfer it
  writes the channel's clock divider, control, timing, address, lengths and
  interrupt mask; on a channel it writes `0x5` rather than `0x1` to the FIFO
  address clear, leaves the acknowledgement-error interrupts masked, sets
  the channel's MCU interrupt enable before the start, returns from a
  completed transfer that was not acknowledged without a reset, and after a
  timeout or an incomplete transfer resets and writes "resume arbitration"
  to channel 0. Its DMA channel for bus 4 is `0x11000100`, the one the
  native node already names.
* *Patch 0006.* `native/patches/0006-i2c-mt65xx-drive-a-channel-of-a-shared-controller.patch`.
  With `mediatek,channel-offset` of `0x100` or `0x200` on a controller with
  the MT8183 register layout, the driver addresses the registers below
  `0x100` at that offset, keeping the controller's own registers above it,
  so no access leaves the node's register block, and logs that it drives the
  controller through that channel. Its reset runs on channel 0, with that
  channel's interrupts masked first, and its configuration is then written
  to channel 0 and to the channel; each transfer clears the multi-channel
  FIFO address, masks the acknowledgement-error interrupts and enables the
  channel's MCU interrupt; a completed transfer that was not acknowledged
  returns without a reset; a timeout or an incomplete transfer resets
  through channel 0 and resumes arbitration, and a timeout also logs a
  warning with the channel's start and status. The patch reads and writes
  the channel through its own accessors, so its interrupt handler never sees
  channel 0. It differs from MediaTek's driver in writing mainline's whole
  configuration, once, rather than the vendor's before each transfer; in
  moving one byte by DMA, where the vendor uses the FIFO for eight bytes or
  fewer; and in relying on the firmware's multi-channel setup, which the
  vendor renews after a suspend, which does not occur here. Without the
  property nothing changes: buses 0 and 1, and the first boot, which enables
  no controller, are unaffected.
* *What the scan should show.* On the channel a missing answer should
  raise only the completion interrupt, as an answer does, with the
  acknowledgement error left in the status, as MediaTek's driver assumes;
  then a working bus raises exactly 112 for the scan. The keyboard
  controller was held in reset in the last trial (GPIO169 low) and the
  cameras are unpowered, so an empty table is the expected answer from a
  working bus; the second AW9523, at `0x5a`, may answer if its reset is free.
  The patterns that are no result:
** About two seconds per address: the channel completes no transfer, or
   gives no completion after a missing answer.
** Fast, 112 interrupts and every address answering: the acknowledgement
   error is not kept in the status, rather than a line held low, since both
   lines read high.
** Fast and stalled transfers alternating, about 56 interrupts: arbitration
   is not resumed after an error.
+
Each points at this implementation, not at the channel as such.
* *Checks.* The kernel check finds the patch in the kernel. The session's
  tree check passes a tree with the status and a 32-bit `0x100` set by
  `fdtput`, and refuses `0x200`, two cells, a string or an extra change; the
  scan verdict refuses the last trial's bus 4 scan and counts of 113 and
  224, and accepts 112 in 0.2 seconds. An independent review of the patch,
  the scripts and this procedure found no blocker; its findings are fixed.

== Preconditions

As in the touch and keyboard bus scan trial, with the latest control-region
copy now the one from its Part 4.

== Procedure

As in the link:native-i2c-input-scan-trial.adoc[touch and keyboard bus scan
trial], with flash profile `ram-nixos-11` and the image
`native-ram-image-v15/UNTESTED-UNSIGNED-native-ram.img`, whose step 7
read-back must show `28680707...`, with the kexec payload's kernel
`1d25af63...`, and with these changes:

* *Step 11:* set `status` to `okay` on `/soc/i2c@11008000` and add
  `mediatek,channel-offset = <0x100>` to it, each checked with `fdtget`
  before and after. The tree check requires the modified tree to differ from
  the running one in exactly those two properties, in the `dtc` sources and
  property by property, with the same reserve map. The marker is
  `titan_kexec=i2c4ch`. The records must show the node reading `okay` and a
  tree that differs from the one handed over only in `/chosen`.
* *Step 12, adapter:* `ls /sys/bus/i2c/devices` must show exactly `i2c-4`,
  and `readlink -f` must end in `/11008000.i2c/i2c-4`; the kernel log must
  show the controller driven through channel `0x100`.
* *Step 12, pins:* save the lines for GPIO2, GPIO105, GPIO106 and GPIO169.
  The scan runs only if GPIO105 and GPIO106 are in function 1, by MediaTek's
  rule, and read input level 1, read again just before it.
* *Step 12, scan:* save the controller's line of `/proc/interrupts`, run
  `timeout 300 i2cdetect -y -r 4` once, then save the interrupt line, the
  pin lines and the kernel log. A real result finishes with status 0 in
  under two seconds, with the interrupt count risen by exactly 112, at most
  eight answers and no I2C error logged, and both lines must read 1
  afterwards. No other I2C command.
* *Part 4:* as in that trial, the owner checks that the touch screen, the
  keyboard and its backlight work.

== Stop conditions

The touch and keyboard bus scan trial's table applies, read for bus 4 alone,
with its tree rows for the node's status and channel offset, and with its
scan row for this trial's real result: status 0, under two seconds, an
interrupt rise of exactly 112, at most eight answers and no I2C error or
timeout warning logged. A scan that is not a real result ends the I2C part;
the patterns above say what each kind of failure points at.

== Residual risks

As in the touch and keyboard bus scan trial. The patch changes how the
driver addresses this controller: the channel at `0x100` receives the whole
configuration and status clears, the FIFO address clear, the interrupt mask
and the MCU interrupt enable, and channel 0 receives the reset, its interrupt
mask cleared and, after a timeout or an incomplete transfer, "resume
arbitration", as MediaTek's kernel does on the same hardware; the camera
control unit does not run here. A stalled transfer costs two seconds before
that reset.

== What this can and cannot show

Success shows the native kernel driving the keyboard's bus, the start for the
keyboard controller once its reset is released, which needs its own trial.
It does not show the keyboard, the cameras or the touch panel working.
